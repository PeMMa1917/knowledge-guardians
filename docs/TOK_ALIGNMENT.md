The examiner reports and examples repeatedly distinguish a memorable item from an item whose particular context does real work. They also distinguish naming concepts from using them to evaluate evidence or explain a consequence. Write feedback about the thing the player did. Show what their claim helps the next person do, what it leaves open, and what could change it. The route audit tracks opportunities to use concepts for development review.

## From arrival to a harder case

Give players an early action they can take for a person in the story. On return, change the material, tool, source or audience and ask what the earlier approach can still do. The opening should give players a foothold for recurring ideas even if they follow different paths through the hall.

| Opening action | How the practice changes later |
|---|---|
| Keep the visitor’s notebook open to compare cases or leave a numbered record another reader can request. | Match a specific clerk’s stub and carrier’s strip in Emberford. Later the Nile keepers use two different records that mention Amunnakht. The records support different kinds of attribution. |
| Turn over the handling seal and post the building date beside the narrower check. | Find where public copies inherit an entry restriction in the City of Copies. Trace where the rule actually came from and choose how to repair the copies people still use. |
| Hold the model pen steady and swap surfaces. See what the marks can support about this bench test. | Move the silence in the theatre while keeping the spoken lines and movement steady. In the Market hold the lots and prices fixed, then change what the scale counts. At the weir repeat a selected setting after fixing the leak, with the inflow held steady. A different claim needs a different check. |
| Pick what a visitor sees first on the entrance guide or where a supported label leaves room for a second reading. | Follow names from the Cartographers’ Sea route book to surveys and readers with different jobs. In the Market make unit rates, grading rules and whole-lot prices useful to different buyers. |
| Follow two readers approaching the same arrow from opposite sides. Install the directions they can use. | In the Nile Archive try the keeper’s slip under two lights, then send the limitations with either the desk note or the travelling copies. At the Water Commons compare what an early fix actually changed for two crews. |
| Use the Date-Kind Lens and a source card to question what “first” covers. | In the Cold Store, use object-specific records to handle a break in custody and an excavated context. Later compare whether the City’s dated trial and signed entries support the same kind of conclusion. |

Authors can use these sequences when reviewing linked DP subjects and practice for the core. The opening model bench gives students a repeatable comparison they can revisit in design, physics or the mathematical work of the Market. The opening record trail lets a reader reconstruct where a claim came from. Later evidence trails give practice comparing and documenting sources for an independent investigation. A useful service moment begins with a person’s need, leads to an agreed action and makes room for that person’s response. The queue and the later buyer’s note give one example. Keep tracks like these around the opening as well as in new worlds, including recurring opportunities for turn-taking, planning and revision. Use journal moments to notice a learner attribute in an action.

## Teaching use

- Let players try, compare and revise. Give feedback through materials, actions, readers and visible map changes.
- Offer multiple ways into the same idea. A player might inspect a physical record, run a controlled trial, follow a route, or return to listen to a reader. Keep key information in text as well as diagrams and sound.
- Keep fictional models labelled, and give documented objects a specific source. For classroom transfer, invite students to choose different examples from their learning or surroundings and explain what those examples actually show.
- Use the current guide for formal assessment requirements and academic integrity. The game provides practice in reasoning and communication. A play route, its credit and its coverage report describe activity in the game.

| Idea to revisit | Early play | A later use that changes the problem |
|---|---|---|
| What a measure leaves out | The Nexus loan tray records a transaction while a waiting tally records people who need a support. Move the supports and find out who can use them. | At the Civic console change which requests count while holding the rule fixed. Then change the rule with the same requests. At the Weir keep intake steady after repair, then compare that result with separate records of what the crews agreed to do. |
| Who is responsible for a record that people rely on | Keep a publicly checkable reference for the model while protecting a detail a reader does not need. | A seat request can justify a public reservation without naming its reader. The Civic plan keeps the inputs and rule visible while opening a way to report a need the counter missed. |
| When a shared wording helps someone act | Compare the Nexus translators’ proofs and choose which context travels with the note. | The Archive of Voices keeps an approved short phrase and the guide it informs. A player walks both paths to find where a common wording needs a named starting point or a shared landmark. |
| How an expert can revise a strong belief | Put a limited claim in front of Clerk Venn and reframe the claim in the Showdown when another piece of evidence changes it. | At the Market or Civic desk retain the earlier result and the evidence that revised it. Update where the next reader actually uses the notice. |
| What changing a model assumption can reveal | In the Nexus marking test change one material at a time. | The Island workshop repeats a delivery plan after a smaller ferry landing changes capacity. Compare the effect on time and on the number of passages through quiet strips. A later observation has a time and place of its own. |

For political and technological questions, give players an identifiable decision-maker, a route by which a claim or rule travels, and somebody who cannot use the result as written. For history and the arts, give records and performances distinct purposes. A recorded date, a remembered experience and a staged cue can all matter to an investigation for different reasons. Avoid giving them one common test of reliability.
