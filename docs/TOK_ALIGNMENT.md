## What players can practise

| Learning priority | Practice in the game | Check during review |
|---|---|---|
| Stay with the full claim and the question it raises | At the City of Copies press, find where an observation loses its trial condition. At the landing, trace the loss of its source. Restore both and give the separate entry rule its own line. | Can the player explain why checking a trial’s time cannot settle who may enter? |
| Use a particular object for a reason | In the Cold Store gallery, the royal custody panel and the Chinchorro records make different contributions. The displays keep separate sites, claims and open questions. Visitors can prompt a revision. | Would swapping objects or labels change what the exhibit can support? Could a player point to a specific feature or record that makes the difference? |
| Compare standards and methods across settings | In the Nexus compare marks on paper and wood before choosing which reader sees them first. In the House of Performance move the pause and hold the spoken lines and crate move steady. | What was held steady, and what kind of conclusion does that make possible in this setting? |
| Evaluate perspectives with reasons | The same scene can look different from two seats. Two charts of the same shoreline serve people doing different work and show distinct limits. Players choose which view to foreground and make the other available. | Does each point of view have a reason grounded in what its reader can see, needs or values? Does it affect what a player puts on display? |
| Keep uncertainty and evidential limits visible | In the Living Forest a clear beat with zero sightings differs from a blocked beat. At the Cartographers’ Sea the survey’s blank area stays unexamined. In the copper case two possible material routes stay open. | Does the response distinguish the record that is missing from a warranted conclusion that something did not happen? |
| Follow an implication into a new case | Revisit the City’s signed entry register after the theatre programme has been revised. A stage action supports an observation while an actor’s motive can remain open. A signed rule can settle an admission question that a trial log could not. | Can the player carry a useful question into a new setting and choose evidence appropriate to that setting? |
| Take responsibility for how a claim travels | An archive seal, display position or delivery route can give a claim authority. At doors and benches, a readable note and a checkable source can change who can use it. | Who can check the claim after publication? What did the next reader infer, and what can the player do about it? |

The examiner reports and examples repeatedly distinguish a memorable item from an item whose particular context does real work. They also distinguish naming concepts from using them to evaluate evidence or explain a consequence. Write feedback about the thing the player did. Show what their claim helps the next person do, what it leaves open, and what could change it. The route audit tracks opportunities to use concepts for development review.

## Teaching use

- Let players try, compare and revise. Give feedback through materials, actions, readers and visible map changes.
- Offer multiple ways into the same idea. A player might inspect a physical record, run a controlled trial, follow a route, or return to listen to a reader. Keep key information in text as well as diagrams and sound.
- Keep fictional models labelled, and give documented objects a specific source. For classroom transfer, invite students to choose different examples from their learning or surroundings and explain what those examples actually show.
- Use the current guide for formal assessment requirements and academic integrity. The game provides practice in reasoning and communication. A play route, its credit and its coverage report describe activity in the game.
