# The travelling pocket theatre

Two small streets fit inside each district’s suitcase. A first direction is optional. The two slides change what the player can see or hear. Keeping one slide still makes a comparison possible. Either public ending can be sent once the player has tried three settings. The ending unlocks a distinct act at the paired desk. After that act the first desk can open its reply. Players can restage a choice and try the other route.

The thirty cases contain 120 different readings, sixty endings, sixty working deliveries, and sixty return reactions. The same 30 cases are available through keyboard, pointer and touch. An optional note follows all four views and an issued ending. Its reference is added to the diary at the moment the note is opened. First person entries, reflection prompts, existing export filters and the class adventure’s disclosed writing rules apply.

## Where the fallacies and biases enter

| In one district | In another | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sefa covers the first lamp price in Emberford. | A market keeper weighs a basket empty. | A price can anchor a bargain. Two weights can refer to different goods. A working sheet supplies a specific comparison at the other desk. |
| A city manager signs a note about folding Tuesday out of sight. | Wren winds a clipped reel back to the initial breath. | Reinstating missing context alters both readings. One desk can establish who deliberately hid a line. The other leaves the cutter’s purpose open. |
| A collector chooses bright intact feathers. | Only some crews can return the harbour keeper’s lantern. | Both collections lose information during selection. The crew and the collector can exchange an actual method to record missing observations. |
| A child sees a stage bird hang in the air. | Two labelled bowls come from one pot. | A cue can guide attention or expectation. One observer’s experience can be faithfully described even when its proposed explanation needs another check. |
| The metal yard tries a lid and a pot together. | A captain wants another voyage to justify three tins of varnish. | A good result from one trial can conceal a problem with the assembly. A purchase can also affect which evidence a planner wants to see. |

The inventory records 45 biases or related effects, 36 argument patterns and ten authored forms of deliberate deception. A name in more than one poster earns one inventory entry. Related phenomena can require different evidence. A social tendency to follow a crowd, an argument from popularity and a signed admission that votes were fabricated have separate records.

Run `npm test -- tests/pocket-theatre.test.ts` for actual scene transitions and the 71 evidence checks. Run `node tools/audit-cognitive-weave.mjs --dimension deception:omission --unit chapter --evidence` to inspect the relevant joint entries. Attributed motive requires an authored observation and its stated basis. The ledger credits opportunities to examine the evidence. A classroom judgement of learning or enjoyment requires student work or observed play.

In the first district-by-district pass, fifteen of the biases, five of the fallacies and two forms of deception appear in at least two separately authored local cases. The rest currently have one reviewed local case each. Travelling sheets add different contexts. The count above keeps them separate from the reviewed local observations. The ninety-one entries form a bounded working inventory, with room to distinguish further forms as new stories are written.
