# Lantern workshops and class writing

## Review decision

Keep this build in draft. A teacher’s access depends on the student’s accepted class assignment. In local play a pre-existing note stays on the device. School deployment still needs account lifecycle operations, an actual hosting pilot and evidence from students and teachers. This is a modeled review from 73 perspectives. There were no participant interviews, examiner endorsements or community approvals in this review.

## What a reviewer can play

At the folded-wing post, call a historical guest. Set two pieces in the guest’s instrument, try reversing their order, then decide which tracing to carry. Walk to the receiving lamp and let the keeper use it. Look for a second groove after bringing the two guests listed below. See how each view changes when laid over the other and try either consequence. The guest remembers an earlier chapter’s handover. All 250 guests are callable in every chapter. Each has an authored home commission, readings and two responses. The eight instrument families share a two-piece interaction. The fifteen paired scenes have their own endings.

The chapter census below includes all indexed missions. The compare-and-handover count and average objective length come from the repository’s text heuristic. They measure neither story quality nor a student’s reading ability. The playtest can use debug travel to a chapter, then checks the pathfinder, the physical props and the ordinary interaction controls inside that location.

## Decisions, trade-offs and open evidence

| Perspectives in tension | Current decision and outstanding check |
| --- | --- |
| Teachers want continuity. Students may want an audience of one. | Named disclosure precedes the class adventure. Local play has its own save. Test whether students can predict the reader before they type. |
| An examiner values a finished argument. A parent may want timely reassurance. | The dashboard identifies work in progress as Draft. Separate portfolio sharing still takes a per-entry choice and file handoff. Discuss classroom expectations before a school pilot. |
| Explorers want the full roster. Readers on small screens may prefer one immediate relationship. | Each chapter’s pair comes first and the roster is searchable. Observe whether players find the receiving bench without help. |
| Achievers want complete coverage. Teachers need evidence of transfer. | The skill ledger counts reachable actions. A separate audit counts the ten-per-world requirement. Validate learning in unfamiliar examples outside the game. |
| Writers want emotional stakes. Historians and community reviewers need specific attribution. | Keep imagined dialogue distinct in the records, expose biographical sources and invite review of each particular portrayal. |
| Sound can make a tool memorable. Some learners need silence or text. | The score rack has both cue tones and a written sequence. Test comprehension in both modes and with assistive technology. |

## Evidence and remaining work

The classroom test uses fictional students and teachers in independent browser profiles. It exercises audience disclosure, saved notes, unfinished writing, student recovery, separation of the photo album and portfolio, an offline edit, conflicting writes and sign-out. The service test additionally exercises replays, five-revision retention and revoked membership. The gameplay tests exercise both routes for each guest and chapter and both endings for each chapter pair. These checks do not measure student attainment, cultural reception or compatibility with every physical device.

The new source adds 200 definitions in ten domains. Forty-seven have reviewed links to played actions in this update. Across all six blocks, 485 of 1,200 skills have an authored play link, 300 in more than one world. The other 715 require evidence. The world-by-element census still has a substantial gap against the ten-action target. The same brief action repeated in multiple sites does not demonstrate independent mastery. An action-by-action evaluation with actual students and all 73 stakeholder groups remains outstanding.
