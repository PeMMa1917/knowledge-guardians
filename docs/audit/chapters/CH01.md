# Chapter 1: Emberford and the Nexus

Chapter 1 covers WORLD-01 (the Nexus) and WORLD-02 (Emberford in Aethelgard). It has 94 missions, 7 of the 9 field games, all 15 puzzles and 2 of the 3 Showdowns (numbers from [EVIDENCE.md](../EVIDENCE.md)). It is the best-made chapter and the one that most players see, so small problems here reach every student.

Persona codes (A1, B5, E2 and so on) are defined in [PERSONAS.md](../PERSONAS.md). Fix numbers (`C-###`) are in [CONSENSUS.md](../CONSENSUS.md). Open questions (`X-###`) are in [CONTESTED.md](../CONTESTED.md). A tick (✅) means the fix is in this branch.

## Whole chapter

### What most personas like

- **The first hour has a real hook.** Three exhibits all say "The first ballpoint pen". The player sees at once that a label can be true in one sense and false in another (M01). Students (A1, A7), TOK teachers (B1), examiners (C1) and philosophers (D2) all name this as the strongest TOK opening in the game.
- **The core cast is distinct.** Ise is short and dry. Corvan has a reason for his claim ("a family to feed"). Hesper speaks plainly and lets the billet speak. Writers (E2, E4) and story-seekers (G5) like this.
- **Play variety is high.** Seven field games and fifteen puzzles sit here. Puzzle solvers (G8), explorers (G2) and casual players (G11) have something new on most streets.
- **No hidden right answer.** Showdowns reward a reply that fits the evidence, not one fixed opinion (OPEN-001). Assessment experts (C3) and philosophy teachers (D1) like this.

### What most personas are concerned about

- **Volume.** 94 missions is far more than any other chapter. Low-motivation students (A5), casual players (G11), learning support (I3) and speedrunners (G9) expect to lose the main thread. The main story is 3 missions (M01, M02, LAMP). Most of the rest are optional reading-room and handover missions.
- **The standard loop.** 14 to 36 per cent of missions in a chapter repeat "compare two records, set one out, a reader uses it" (EVIDENCE.md). In chapter 1 many R- and S-series missions have this shape. Game designers (E1), critics (F2) and engagement researchers (H4) expect it to feel the same after the fourth time.
- **Paperwork words.** Dialogue uses "slip" 1,060 times, "bell" 692, "rail" 566, "strip" 348. EAL students (A4), dyslexic players (I2) and linguists (J3) say the words are short but their meaning in the world is not clear.
- **Step titles that named objects only by number.** Before this audit, 405 step titles named a museum object by its accession number only (for example "Fit a repeatable view beside 2005.100.494.13."). All personas agreed this reads like a database, not a world. ✅ Fixed (C-003).
- **The core cast speaks rarely after the opening.** The busiest speakers are reading-room keepers (Aven 293 scenes, Noll 241, Merrit 217). Ise has 49 and Kip 36. Story-seekers (G5) and writers (E2) want Kip, Hesper and Sefa to come back in the side missions with their own opinions.

## 1. Arrival and Lumen

Files: `src/content/m01-nexus.json`, `src/content/welcome.json`.

- **A1, G5 like** the glowing label that the player sees first. It is a question, not a lecture.
- **A4, I2, J3 concern:** the welcome scenes use words that the game has not explained yet ("velin", "clamp tray"). **Solution:** Lumen's glossary underlines these words, or the first scene uses plain words and the world word comes second.
- **D2, C3, B1 concern:** Lumen's first line said "I will not give you the 'right' opinion. I don't have one." Philosophers say this mixes two things. Some claims are facts that can be checked. Others are judgements. The line could make a student think that every question is only opinion. **Solution:** Lumen says it helps check facts, and the judgements belong to the player. ✅ Fixed (C-026): "I can help you check facts. Judgements are yours, and I will not make one for you."
- **K1, I1 like** the H key and the lantern button for hints. **K1 concern:** the button is small on a phone. See C-013.
- **X-011 decided:** a setting, on by default. Lumen gives a small sign, and the hint shows only when the student clicks.

## 4. M02 The Label on the Blade

Mission `KG-CH1-M02`. Stake: "families pay for a story".

- **Likes:** D7 and B3 (chemistry, design) like the real metallurgy question: a surface pattern can come from more than one way of making. E2 and E4 like Corvan, because his reason is human, not evil. B8 likes that money and fairness are part of it without any betting.
- **Concerns:** A3 and A5 say the Provenance Thread has three stops and the reason for each stop is not always said aloud. J2 and D7 ask whether "ancient" pattern-welding traditions are named with care.
- **Solutions:** Corvan needs to make rent, Kip knows his aunt’s pay date, and Hesper dates the work that leaves her hands. Their records establish three different things. Cultural care note: names of real traditions follow AGENTS.md rule 17, and the content note list (C-014) covers this blade.

## 10. The East Well

Mission `KG-CH1-S02`. Efua Asante, the fruit seller, Hesper, and knotted ropes at two wells.

- **Likes:** D8 and B15 like the question "Whose knowledge should shape a plan that affects a whole community?" E2 likes Efua.
- **Concerns:** J1 and J2 note that knotted ropes used as records can remind players of the khipu of the Andes. If so, the game should not borrow a real people's record system without care. The game says the ropes are local to Emberford.
- **X-014 decided:** the ropes are now notched tally sticks, with a notch for every hand of depth. ✅ Lumen's "tally" entry has an example for the east well.

## 11. The Shattering Rumour

Mission `KG-CH1-R01`. Idris, Hana, the public record book, the tea seller.

- **Likes:** A1, D8 and B1 like a rumour traced back to a first source. It is a strong media-literacy lesson without news content.
- **Concerns:** it is a fetch chain (ask, ask, read, tell). E1 says a branching chain would be better, where the player chooses whom to ask next and can reach a wrong first source.
- **Solutions:** Hana offers the register or the four chalk marks. Each mark leads to the same porter. Either path can reach the original record, and the Guardian can direct the correction to the kettle, the late shift or a margin beside the evidence.

## 14. Reading rooms and handovers (the R- and S-series)

About 60 optional missions: R09 to R99, S05 to S18, the ferry copy, the Fourth Corner, the departure strip, and others. Most have one to three steps.

- **Likes:** G2 and G6 like the density of real museum objects. D7 and B3 like how many subjects appear (chemistry of pigment, physics of light, history of print). B12 likes that each one has a knowledge question and often a stake.
- **Concerns:**
  - The standard loop (compare, set out, a reader uses it) repeats. R09, R10, R18, S06, S07, S10, S12, R23, R24, R84, R96 all have it (EVIDENCE.md).

- **Solutions:**
  - ✅ Plain titles for the hardest steps (C-003). R05 is now "Choose how Iven's lamp crate is called for, then follow the crate to see who gets it."
  - C-005 voice sheet: each keeper gets a belief and a worry. Example of what the sheet could say (not yet written): Aven at the field store believes a label must survive rain, and Noll at the reference line worries that readers never ask for help.
  - C-016: some of these missions end with the player making a weighed judgement that a character challenges, instead of a reader using a display.
  - C-017: the peer apprentice sometimes disagrees with the player's choice.
