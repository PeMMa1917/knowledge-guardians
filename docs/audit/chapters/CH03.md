## Whole chapter

### What most personas like
- **A hard, real subject handled as knowledge.** How people cared for their dead, told through two records that must not be read as one story. Historians (D7), inclusion researchers (I4) and TOK teachers (B1) say the main question ("How can a public record name what it knows and still leave room for a missing link?") is one of the best in the game.
- **The stake is human.** If the gap in the file is hidden, a family loses its claim to an ancestor's name (E1, G5).
- **Yolŋu records treated as records.** The Dhuwa and Yirritja stations (the two moieties of the Yolŋu people of north-east Arnhem Land) show a petition receipt that does not claim to know the outcome. J1 and J2 like that the record leaves space for the people's own account.
- **The Luba lukasa.** The memory board station separates what a board shows from what only its keeper can say (D7, J1).

### What most personas are concerned about
- **No content note before human remains.** Families (B9), counsellors (I4) and Indigenous-studies reviewers (J1) ask for a note and a way to step out (X-005, C-014). ✅ Fixed: the royal and Chinchorro rooms show a short note the first time, with sources and a "Step back" button. Nothing is lost by stepping back.
- **Accession numbers in step titles.** The two-rail gallery (R21) had titles like "Check the viewing reply for the paint box of vizier amenemope (1914.680)" (C-003). ✅ Fixed: "Hear what a viewer says about “Paint Box of Vizier Amenemope”", and the audit test now catches numbers in brackets too.
- **The Missing Face (R42) repeats one action 37 times.** "Make a two-view mount for …" then "Leave the supported views … in the Nile Archive". Same problem as the chapter 2 Workbench (E1, F2, A5).
- **The highest standard-loop share so far (0.36).** Many missions end with "send a slip and check what its reader uses" (E1, G8).
- **Keepers agree too much.** Odile and Luz have clear jobs but no argument with each other (E2, E3).
