## Whole chapter

### What most personas like
- **Forecasting as a TOK case.** A reading, a model estimate and a revised notice are three different kinds of claim. Physics and geography teachers (B3), scientists (D3) and TOK teachers (B1) call "The Next Bell" the clearest lesson on uncertainty in the game.
- **Real instruments and real observers.** The Rasulid astrolabe of ‘Umar ibn Yusuf (Yemen, 13th century), Henrietta Leavitt's plates, and the Timbuktu sky records give many traditions of observation (D7, I4, J2).
- **Star Pictures.** One sky, many pictures, each carrying real knowledge (G8, H4).

### What most personas are concerned about
- **The keepers never disagree.** Neri and Ruan pass the notice between them politely (E2, E3, G5). ✅ Fixed: four two-reply moments (X-008), after the choice of how to carry the forecast's limit and after the choice of how to send the notice.
- **Long abstract step titles.** The bearing room (R26) said "Trace two same-bell sights and fit a route aid beside the record of …" twelve times (A3, A4, I2). ✅ Fixed: "Take two sights at the same bell and find where they cross on …" and "Look again at … when the light or weather has changed."
- **Too many "reference" missions.** R22 (The Line That Stayed) has seven steps about a fixed reference, and R20, R77 and R78 teach close ideas (E1, G8).
- **A Lid with Two Names (R21) has nine unrelated steps** (pressure, water, counting units). Players lose the thread (A5, G11).
