# Chapter 2: The Nile archive

Chapter 2 is WORLD-03. The player goes through the Door Without a Name into an archive of the Nile valley. The keepers are Archivist Imani, Nasir (archive copies), Leila (the workers' reading room) and Marek (the route model). The chapter has 31 missions. Its standard-loop share is 0.26, with 1 play-shaped mission and no field games or puzzles of its own ([EVIDENCE.md](../EVIDENCE.md)).

Persona codes are in [PERSONAS.md](../PERSONAS.md). Fix numbers are in [CONSENSUS.md](../CONSENSUS.md). Mika's decisions are in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md). A tick (✅) means the fix is in the game.

## Whole chapter

### What most personas like
- **Real, hard history with real sources.** The chapter uses the Battle of Qadesh (a royal account and a later treaty), the strike recorded by the scribe Amunnakht in year 29 of Ramesses III, and the mine map of Wadi Hammamat. Historians (D7), TOK teachers (B1) and history teachers (B3) say this is the clearest "date of the event versus date of the record" lesson in the game.
- **The packet choice in the main mission** (`nile.dispatch`). The player chooses where the first packet goes: the public case, the reading table or the workshop. Each choice reaches a different audience. Game designers (E1) and role-players (G10) like that it changes what Imani does next.
- **Workers' voices beside the king's.** Citizenship researchers (D8) and inclusion researchers (I4) like that the strike account gives ordinary workmen a voice in the archive.

### What most personas are concerned about
- **No play of its own.** Chapter 2 had no field game and no puzzle. Puzzle solvers (G8), casual players (G11) and engagement researchers (H4) expect the chapter to feel like reading after the variety of chapter 1. ✅ It now has The Copyists' Ladder (C-004).
- **The Workbench room.** "A Turn at the Workbench" (`KG-CH2-R45`) has 36 steps of "Compare two work methods for …". The objects come from many peoples (Iznik, Kashan, Mamluk, Gujarat, Sumba, Mandaya, Joseon, Faenza and others). Explorers (G2) like the range. Writers (E2) and critics (F2) say it repeats one action 36 times and breaks the feeling of a Nile archive.
- **Knowledge questions that were instructions.** Five "earlier viewing" stations and the Weaver star path used an instruction as their knowledge question ("Turn the Map Room's old counter sketch against the same landmarks."). ✅ Fixed game-wide (C-028): all 22 such missions now ask a real question, and a test keeps it so.
- **Keepers explain more than they feel.** Imani, Nasir, Leila and Marek are clear, but they rarely say what they want or fear (E2, E3, G5).

## Encounters

### 1. M01 The Door Without a Name
- **Likes:** the stake (a king's boast becoming fact in the Nexus) is strong and concrete (C1, D7). The return to the Nexus gives the player a reason to carry the packet (E1).
- **Concerns:** "Follow two leads" does not say which leads. A3 and A5 expect players to wander.
- **Solutions:** the objective already updates as leads are found. No change. ✅ Two replies after the packet choice show why each audience matters.

### 2. Two Dates at Qadesh (`KG-CH2-R01`) and The Seal on the Copy (`R09`)
- **Likes:** D7, C1 and B3: a royal poem and a later treaty make "date of the event, date of the record, and whose record" concrete. Nasir's line "It looked so tidy" is one of the better keeper lines.
- **Concerns:** J2 asks whether the Hittite side of the treaty is visible, so that the lesson is not only about Egyptian records.
- **Solutions:** planned: one line from Nasir that the treaty survives in both an Egyptian and a Hittite version, with the source. This also follows C-024 (each record checks the other).

### 3. The Workmen's Thread (`R02`), A Name in Two Places (`R11`), The Reading Window (`R08`)
- **Likes:** D8 and I4: the strike record. A7: "a name can link two records that do different jobs" is a subtle TOK point.
- **Concerns:** R02 and R08 use the standard loop (choose, then leave a note for the next reader).
- **Solutions:** ✅ plain titles. ✅ Two replies after the Amunnakht filing (Leila: a name is how people search; Marek: some links should be harder to make).

### 4. Model and route missions: A Map Is Not a Road (`R03`), The Tilt Tray (`R10`), Both Ends of the Drawing (`R13`), The Bend That Stayed (`KG-CH2-BEND`)
- **Likes:** B3 (physics, geography) and H3: a model lets you decide some things and not others. The Bend That Stayed is a real materials test (a change that lasts versus one that depends on the test).
- **Concerns:** R10 and R13 use the standard loop. E1: the three model missions feel alike.
- **Solutions:** C-016: one of them should end with Marek challenging the player's decision. Planned.

### 5. The Workbench (`R45`)
- **Likes:** G2, G6 and B3 (visual arts, design): real objects and real making methods.
- **Concerns:** 36 identical steps (E1, F2, A5). The location does not fit the Nile archive (E2).
- **Solutions:** C-010 drawer grouping. A framing line from Imani on why a Nile archive keeps a workbench of objects from many places (a travelling makers' exchange). Planned.

### 6. Earlier viewing stations (north turn, horizon copy, earliest line, portable latitude, telescope rim)

- **Concerns:** their knowledge questions were instructions.
- **Solutions:** ✅ real questions (C-028).

### 7. The Spare Seat, the Runner's Second Line (`R14`), the Corner That Moved (`R15`), Where the Copy Stops (`R16`)
