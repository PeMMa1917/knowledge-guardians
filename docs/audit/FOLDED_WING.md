# The folded-wing post • persona audit

Knowledge Guardians • Release review • 27 November 2026

The existing game-wide review covers the campaign chapter by chapter. This follow-up examines the new correspondence stories, the settings and the returning keeper. The 73 perspectives below are modeled design reviews. They do not represent interviews, community consent, institutional endorsement or measured student attainment.

## Play this route

Begin beside Warden Ise in the Archive Hall. Unfold Gārgī’s bird, decide what to do with the thread and read the destination on the fold. At the Nile the voice reports what happened to that particular thread. The keeper asks what to do with it. On returning home you can reopen the source or find the correspondent named on the other half of the watermark.

## Release findings

- **Story.** The supplied 140 people have 140 distinct predicaments, 280 first decisions with individually written consequences, and 70 matched stories across 15 chapter posts. A second, local decision determines who gets to use the first decision. Chapter travel still follows the existing story gates.
- **What learners can try.** Close Ibn al-Haytham’s shutter or move his screen. Move the observer or ball in Wang Zhenyi’s eclipse. Vary cash and debt independently with Brahmagupta. Move a scrape in a four-beat sound reel with Genesis P-Orridge. All four settings on each bench can travel to the next keeper.
- **Existing field quests.** Mozi at the Old Weir, Ibn al-Haytham at Headland Station and Ibn Khaldun in the Nile Map Room also have in-person three-test quests. The scholar and the letter share a single journal identity. Bringing back a finished fold changes their reply according to the actual first decision.
- **Feedback and recovery.** Every delivery names the destination, allows a revision before a keeper acts and provides four depths of optional help per objective. A previously handled reply can be reconsidered at the receiving post. The matching watermark waits until both latest replies have been kept.
- **Student record.** The diary has 140 case-specific optional reflection questions. Encountered historical speakers have article links and MLA citations in the diary and its exports. Student writing stays with the selected entries. Day, week and month streak displays are each opt-in and independent of leaderboard sharing.
- **Checks.** 2,447 automated tests passed across 290 files. All 560 combinations of the sender’s and keeper’s choices ran through the game rules. Separate tests cover both halves of all 70 matches, all 16 bench settings and old-save restoration. Navigation stress exercised all 128 maps and a simulated hour of pushing against a solid wall.
- **Browser play.** Browser interaction covered all 15 posts across desktop Chromium, tablet-width Firefox and phone-width Linux WebKit. Three pairs were played through their matched ending. A separate Chromium check downloaded a Word diary and generated a native PDF with a student-written reflection and the encountered article link. The Nile regression checked each individual prerequisite, two independent investigations, the dispatch and its return display. Test fixtures moved between chapters and placed the player near the posts. The station interaction, menus, decisions, return state, controls and streak settings used the running game.
- **Learning evidence.** Six documented actions exercise deductive, inductive, abductive, analogical, causal and pragmatic reasoning. The game-wide audit now records 15 explicit comparisons between two areas. It verifies ten distinct actions in 2 of 9,327,648 world–dimension–element combinations. Another 333 have one to nine checked actions.
- **Source and review gaps.** 359 of the 803 supplied numbered skill definitions have an authored gameplay link. The five files omit 197 numbered definitions. The public Drive index contains 931 files in 55 folders. Indexing establishes what is available. Lesson-level adaptation, representative classroom trials, real assistive devices and reviews with individual communities remain incomplete.

## Decisions where players may want different things

- **Mystery and wayfinding.** An explorer may enjoy chasing an address from memory. A player returning after a week may need a direct cue. The destination is named on the fold and the last optional hint explains the route. Test long-gap returns before adding permanent markers.
- **Revisable decisions and narrative stakes.** Players may want to change their minds while others want a neighbour to remember a promise. The first fold can change before delivery. Later, the keeper can try a different way of circulating it. The player hears the new reply before the watermark fits again.
- **Visibility and stewardship.** A publicly readable route helps a stranger find the work. Private access can protect a participant or an unnamed maker. These choices receive equal credit. A source check and community-specific review should precede wider use.
- **Motivation and pressure.** A daily streak can help one student return and discourage another after an absence. Day, week and month displays open independently and start switched off. A hosted, public class ranking needs separate review.
- **Breadth and depth.** A chapter can offer many correspondents while four working benches allow more direct experimentation. Choose the next bench by observed learner difficulty and the dramatic stakes of the case. A count of dialogue branches cannot establish enjoyment or transfer.
