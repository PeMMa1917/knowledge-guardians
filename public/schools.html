Knowledge Guardians for schools and families

A Guardian follows clues, tries out tools and decides whose account to carry forward. Two students can leave the same room with different stories. What a student encounters can give a teacher a place to start a conversation. It cannot predict an assessment grade.

Who can read a student’s writing?

Local adventure
Choose New game or Continue on the title screen. Progress and writing stay in this browser. Anyone with access to the same unlocked browser profile may be able to open the adventure. A student decides when to export or hand over a file.
Class adventure
On a school installation with class saves, sign in and check the teacher and class shown before entering. The student and that class’s assigned teacher can read saved diary notes, reflections, writing begun in scenes and unfinished writing. Drafts have a Draft label. Classmates and teachers assigned only to other classes have no writing view.
Class nicknames and scores
Offline results stay personal. In an online stitching round the school server counts moves and measures time. Students choose whether to share a result with their class and can withdraw it. Their assigned teacher can review rejected nicknames, replace or hide a nickname, change a display name, or issue a replacement sign-in username for the same saved adventure.
Portfolio and photos
Students choose which separate portfolio entries to share in a progress file. The assigned teacher’s writing dashboard does not include private portfolio entries. Private portfolio text can travel in the student’s personal recovery save. Photos stay on the device. A student may separately choose what to include when handing over an export.

Moving to a different device

Use the same class account. On the first device, wait for “Synced to your class.” On the next device, sign in, check the class and teacher, and choose Continue. A pending save can still be on the first device. If both devices have edited the same save, reopen the class adventure and compare the two copies before choosing. Local and class adventures have separate saves.

What the school hosts

The local edition downloads game files from the host and saves play in the browser. It needs no account. The optional class service uses a same-origin Cloudflare Worker and D1 database for accounts, accepted class memberships, sessions and saved revisions. Students use a pseudonymous username and a password. The game has no advertising, analytics or runtime AI. Opening an optional museum or encyclopedia link takes the reader to that publisher’s site.

The class service is in draft review with fictional accounts. A school evaluating it should distinguish the tested class save and dashboard from planned account recovery, deletion and retention tools. Before using real accounts, the school needs to confirm its hosting location, retention arrangements, access procedures and applicable student-data requirements.

Handing in work

The Adventure Journal can export a chosen part of an adventure, including the student’s own writing. A teacher importing a progress file sees a separate, formative summary and the portfolio entries the student chose to share. A downloaded copy stays with the person who received it.
